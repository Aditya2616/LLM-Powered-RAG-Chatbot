Harbourline Outfitters: Product Care Guide
Version 1.8, effective 1 March 2026. Owner: Product Quality.
This guide is for shop staff to give consistent advice. Customers receive a shorter version in the box.

1. Waterproof shell jackets
Wash at 30 degrees with a technical wash. Never use fabric softener or ordinary detergent, because they clog the membrane. After washing, tumble dry on a low heat for 20 minutes to revive the water-repellent finish. When water stops beading on the surface, re-proof the jacket with a spray-on or wash-in treatment. Heavy users should expect to do this about every 6 months.

2. Down jackets
Wash a down jacket in a front-loading machine at 30 degrees using a down-specific wash. Dry it on a low heat with three clean tennis balls in the drum so that the feathers separate. Drying takes 3 to 4 hours. The jacket must be completely dry before it is stored. Store it loose in the large cotton storage sack, not in the small stuff sack.

3. Sleeping bags
Do not store a sleeping bag compressed for longer than 2 weeks. At home, keep it loose in a large mesh sack. Wash a sleeping bag no more than once a year, because each wash reduces the loft. A liner is the best way to keep a bag clean.

4. Tents
Dry a tent completely within 48 hours of coming home, even if it only rained a little. Never put a damp tent in a bag for storage, because mould will ruin the coating. Clean it with a soft brush and cold water. Do not use detergent or a washing machine. Use all the guy lines when pitching so that the fabric is not stressed. Seal the seams once a year with seam sealer. A footprint groundsheet protects the floor and is recommended.

5. Stoves and gas
Clean the jet of a stove after every 20 hours of use. Check the rubber O-rings at the start of each season and replace them if they are cracked. In temperatures below freezing use a canister with an isobutane and propane blend, because plain butane will not vaporise properly. Never leave a gas canister in a car in hot weather. Do not let it get warmer than 50 degrees.

6. Walking boots
Brush off mud and rinse them after every trip. Dry boots slowly at room temperature, with the insoles removed. Never put them on a radiator or near a fire, because heat damages the glue and cracks the leather. Treat leather boots with wax every 3 months if you use them regularly.

7. Water filters
Backflush a water filter after every trip with clean water. Replace the filter cartridge after 1,500 litres. If it was frozen while it was wet, throw it away, because ice can crack the fibres inside without any visible sign.

8. Rucksacks
Empty all pockets and brush out grit before storing a rucksack. Wash by hand in lukewarm water and let it dry hanging up. Lubricate sticking zips with a small amount of zip wax. Do not machine wash a rucksack.
